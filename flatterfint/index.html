An old Norwegian saying

«Det er i motbakke det går oppover.»

“It's on the uphill that you go up.”

My reply

Jeg vil ikke ha motbakke. Jeg vil ikke oppover. Flatt er fint!

“I don't want the uphill. I don't want to go up. Flat is fine!”

Every coach, every podcast and every poster with an eagle on it is selling you the same thing: a summit. Nobody stops to ask whether you wanted to be up there. It's cold. It's windy. You take one photo and walk back down.

I coach the other thing. Life at ground level, where the cafés are.

Three flat truths.

  1. 01

    Up is a direction. Not a virtue.

    Most of the mountains on your list were put there by someone else: a boss, a parent, a stranger on LinkedIn. We go through them one by one and hand them back.

  2. 02

    Easy isn't cheating.

    When something feels light, that's not a sign you're slacking. It's a sign it fits. We build your days out of more of exactly that.

  3. 03

    Enough is a place you can live.

    Not a base camp on the way to more. An actual address. I help you find it, and then we furnish it.

A word from René Descartes (1596–1650)

Even Descartes stayed on the plane.

The man gave us the Cartesian plane. A plane. Flat in every direction, forever. Four hundred years on, people still call it genius. He also said a few things, and I've read them the way he would have: lying down.

A cautionary tale

For most of his life Descartes stayed in bed until late morning, thinking. It went brilliantly: modern philosophy, analytic geometry. In 1649 he took a job in Stockholm, tutoring Queen Christina at five in the morning, in winter. By February 1650 he was dead. I'm not saying it was the early starts. I'm saying he was fine while he was flat.

What the mountains are saying

Fair questions.

Isn't this just giving up?

No. Giving up is turning back halfway and feeling bad about it. This is choosing the valley on purpose and feeling great about it.

What if I actually want to climb something?

Then climb it. I'll wave from the bottom. The work is telling the mountains you want apart from the ones you were handed.

Will I still grow?

Sideways. There's far more room.

Are you a flat-earther?

No. The Earth is round. It's your calendar that doesn't have to be steep.

Come down from there.

Send me a note and tell me which mountain you're tired of. We'll take it from there. Slowly.
